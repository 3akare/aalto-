What a limited company actually is

A limited company is a legal person in its own right. It can hold money, sign contracts and be taken to court separately from the people who own it. That separation is the whole point, and it is called limited liability.

In practice it means that if the business cannot pay what it owes, the people who own it lose what they put in and no more. Their house is not on the table. A sole trader has no such line: the debts of the business are simply their debts.

Registration is not instant. Expect a decision within five working days of filing, and note that a name can be refused for being too similar to one already on the register, which is the most common reason an application comes back.

Once registered, the company must file accounts every year even if it traded nothing at all. Dormant accounts are short, but they are not optional, and the penalty for filing late starts at a hundred and fifty pounds.

What the agent is doing

    Seven things you can say

    Each of these is a tool the agent can reach for on its own. One sentence often uses several of them, and you can interrupt at any point.

    Dictate anywhere

    Speak into any box on any site. It writes what you meant, punctuated, in the register of wherever it is going - and edits what is already there when you ask.

    "Actually, make that shorter and less formal."

    Fill any form

    One sentence fills several fields. It reads every answer back including the blanks, asks when a value matches no option, and refuses to submit what you have not heard.

    "My name is Ada Bello, I'm in retail, sole proprietorship."

    Ask the page

    Questions about what is in front of you get answered from the page itself, and it scrolls to the sentence it used - instead of dropping you on a results page.

    "How long does registration take here?"

    Drive your tabs

    Open, switch and close by description rather than by hunting. Anything it opens goes behind what you are already reading.

    "Open my inbox behind this and close the research tabs."

    Chain it, and interrupt it

    One instruction can be several actions. If you change your mind halfway through a sentence, say so - it stops talking and takes the new instruction.

    "No - the other one."

    Work on what you selected

    Highlight anything and have it summarised to your clipboard, rewritten in plain language, or replaced in place.

    "Summarise this into three bullets on my clipboard."

    Teach it once

    Save a sequence you do often under a name you choose, then ask for it by that name whenever you want it again.

    "Remember that as my morning setup."

    Install the extension

    Two minutes, and no store listing to wait on. Chrome, Edge or any Chromium browser.

    1. Download and unzip

      aalto-1.0.0.zip - unzip it somewhere you will not delete by accident, like your home folder. The extension loads from that folder, so moving it later means loading it again.

    2. Open your extensions page

      Go to chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode, top right. Chrome only offers the next button once that is on.

    3. Load unpacked

      Click Load unpacked and pick the unzipped aalto folder - the one with manifest.json directly inside it, not its parent.

    4. Grant the microphone once

      Press Alt+A. The first time, Aalto opens a page asking for the microphone - it has to ask from a real page, because the invisible document that does the listening is allowed to use the microphone but not to prompt for it. Say yes once and it will not ask again.

    5. Start talking

      Alt+A anywhere starts a conversation, and pressing it again ends one. You can rebind it at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.
